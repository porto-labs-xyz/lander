# Kiln copy direction

Private, local compute is the reason for the product. The narrative follows an agency’s work from its first brief through exploration and revision, on models hosted on the studio’s own Kiln hardware. Keep the established headlines and a concrete, purposeful paragraph under each.

- **Introduction** — your models, your hardware, your agency’s work kept in the studio.
- **Context** — carry briefs, references and decisions between rounds within a private project.
- **KilnOS** — explore directions, shape storyboards and refine with models running on Kiln. Familiar laptops provide the workspace; the studio’s hardware does the computing.
- **Hardware** — a compact local model/project home in One; expandable rack-ready local compute in Atelier.
- **Privacy** — contracts, accounts, legal documents, unreleased ideas and creative iterations. Explain the local processing boundary and the absence of automatic cloud fallback.
- **Invitation** — shape the private local workflow through forthcoming agency pilots.

The About page connects independent creative judgement to local models and studio-controlled access. Founder facts and roles remain unchanged.

## Privacy claim boundaries

Reviewed against the current KilnOS Harness Engineering Plan v1.2 (12 November 2026), especially the selected access model, model collection and privacy/execution controls, plus the founder’s explicit direction in this copy pass. These are design requirements, not evidence of an implemented or independently verified product.

Local project content belongs on Kiln and authorised studio devices. Model workers must not export project content or silently fall back to cloud processing. Company identity, device policy, provisioning and updates may need internet access. Optional external services require project approval and have separate data terms. Do not promise that all deployments are air-gapped or that nothing ever connects to the internet.

The intended local workflow keeps prompts, files and outputs out of external model-training pipelines. Model use is not itself training. Do not claim that hardware alone enforces privacy; controls require pilot validation. ‘Your models’ means an approved, locally installed collection managed by the studio, subject to licensing, compatibility and memory, not ownership of third-party model IP or universal model support.

Business documents demonstrate why privacy matters; they do not introduce a shipped legal/accounting automation suite. The first qualified pilot remains creative briefs, directions, image storyboards and revisions. FAQs explain these distinctions without burdening the main narrative.

Retain pricing qualifications, in-development status, specifications, website privacy disclosures and form consent. Sites remains a registration preview; Gatsby retains its working enquiry form. No imagery, motion or interaction behavior changes in this pass.
